# Authored Replacement Direction: The Attention Field

## Product decision

Personal Calander is a private operating workspace for someone repeatedly deciding what deserves attention, when it fits, and whether it supports a longer direction. It is **not** a soft self-improvement dashboard. The primary action is to create, complete, reschedule, and place real work without losing a clear view of time or long-horizon context.

The former Mineral Workbench visually changed the application but retained too much of the inherited card-grid grammar. Its pale panels, small labels, and broad empty zones failed to establish a decisive attention order. This replacement moves from a themed dashboard to a **task rail, attention field, and temporal edge**.

| Decision | Replacement rule | Why it is specific to this product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layout | A stable left task rail, central commitment field, and right time edge | Planning requires immediate choice and temporal fit at once. |
| Density | Compact rows, clear dividers, and no decorative containers | Repeated daily use benefits from information density, not marketing whitespace. |
| Typography | Manrope for decision text; DM Mono only for dates, quantities, and system readings | The user needs readable task language plus scannable planning facts. |
| Color | Cool paper and ink with one cobalt command signal; green and amber are semantic only | One action color clarifies commitment without making every route a new themed product. |
| Components | Lines, sheets, and purposeful panels rather than a collection of similar rounded cards | Boundaries indicate ownership, state, or a change of planning mode. |
| Motion | Press feedback and drawer/sheet spatial context only | Planning is frequent work; decoration must not slow it down. |

## Responsive contract

Desktop uses the rail / attention field / temporal edge composition. At tablet widths the rail compresses while the field remains primary. At phone widths, the work field becomes a vertical sequence: context, capture, commitment list, time, then supporting tools. The fixed bottom navigation preserves the user’s established four-direct-destinations-plus-More model and safe-area spacing.

## Constraints preserved

This direction does not change planner data, task lifecycle, category management, task-only Calendar semantics, the separate habit tracker, long-horizon logic, offline task capture and replay, PWA/device controls, private ICS, AI confirmation flow, or the intentionally paused automatic reminder delivery boundary.
